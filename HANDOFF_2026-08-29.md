### 1.1 The arithmetic that makes the frame axis one number (L252)

`frame_candidates()` (`constructive.cpp:737`) builds every candidate as
`w = base·s·√a`, `h = base·s/√a`, `base = √ΣA` ⇒ **frame area = ΣA·s², so
utilisation is exactly 1/s², independent of aspect.** 44 of 55 profile dicts run
the ladder `1.00, 1.05, 1.10, 1.20` = 100 / 90.70 / 82.64 / 69.44 %. The label's
96.6 % is `s = 1.0174` — **between rung 1 and rung 2, where no rung exists.**

Measured on the proxy-winning profile, OOS s1, n ≥ 101, 40 cases, exp(n/12):

| | s | util | area vs label |
|---|---|---|---|
| label | 1.0174 | 96.6 % | — |
| `s_fine` tightest packable (dense ladder) | **1.1088** | **81.3 %** | **+18.82 %** |
| `s_coarse` tightest on the shipped ladder | 1.1222 | 79.4 % | +21.66 % |
| `s_landed` what `layout_score` picks | 1.1303 | 78.3 % | +23.41 % |
| `s_eff` the final bbox | 1.1260 | 78.9 % | **+22.49 %** |

🔑 `s_eff` is computed from raw positions with no label, and reproduces L251's
`area_gap = 0.2256` to **0.07 pp**. The s-view of `area_gap` is validated.

Split: selector slack −1.43 % (9/40 cases), ladder grain −2.38 % (26/40), **cliff
+18.82 %**. Priced on L251's method: **+1.50 % of the +9.18 % area prize.**

⚠️ And that +1.50 % is optimistic: `constructive.cpp:1900` says `max_trials` is
capped at 4 *because* `layout_score`'s 150000×bv weight "picks low-violation but
area-bloated outlines" — so the looser frame is being chosen **to buy
violations**, and L251 already shows vrel is a **surplus**. Re-open this only as
an area/violation trade.

## 3. Traps this session paid for

* **`_l137_env()` is non-empty at ≥40 cores.** Building the binary's stdin with
  `gnn_hint=None` builds a *different case* than the 48c deployment path. The
  byte-comparison gate in `l252_fine.py` caught it on the first run; without it
  the whole dense sweep would have measured wrong inputs and printed a perfectly
  plausible table. Third member of `[[probe-import-time-silent-nooks]]`.
* **`env.update(env_over)` means the profile dict beats the ambient environment**
  (`optimizer_constructive.py:2178-2179`) ⇒ setting `ICCAD_FRAME_SCALES` in the
  shell is a silent no-op for the 44 profiles that set it themselves. Inject into
  the profile dict instead.
* **msys g++ needs `C:\msys64\ucrt64\bin` on PATH**, not just an absolute path to
  the exe — otherwise it exits 1 with **empty stderr**, exactly the shape
  `[[windows-msys-path-silent-sa-fallback]]` records.
* **Only the PRIMARY pack of a frame may be instrumented.** `ORDER_SWAP`'s
  hill-climb and `REFINE`'s guide passes call `pack_in_frame` again; their
  failures look identical to cliff events and are not. `L254_PRIMARY` gates it.
* **A metric can be bounded by construction and look like evidence.** L254's
  `free / (area of ALL unplaced blocks)` reads p50 6.79×, min 1.23×, 30/30 > 1 —
  and proves nothing, because it equals `(tot·s² − placed)/(tot − placed)`, which
  is > 1 for every `s > 1`. Recorded so nobody quotes it. Check whether your
  discriminator *could* have come out the other way.
* **Report medians next to means.** L254's "10.3 blocks left" is one 81/116
  outlier; the median is **3**.

## 4. What is next, and why

The path §3b took to exhaustion was: **iterated frame shrinking with
ruin-and-recreate.** It was the only mechanism the measurements sized correctly:

1. pack at the frame that works (today's `s_min`);
2. shrink the frame one rung;
3. remove the blocks that no longer fit plus their neighbourhood (~10 %);
4. re-place them with the existing candidate/scoring machinery;
5. accept if all placed, else restore and stop.

Why this shape and not the alternatives:

* it **reuses** the shipped candidate generation, scoring, boundary handling and
  MIB/cluster logic — it is a post-pass, not a rewrite;
* its cost is bounded and partly **already paid**: the frames it works on are the
  ones the trial loop currently packs and throws away;
* L255 says its prize is a curve, not a point, so it can be tuned to the runtime
  budget rather than being all-or-nothing;
* the from-scratch alternative was tried — L129's global placer reached 1.745
  against the shipped 1.237, won 2/64 cases, and is unpriced at 2.3 s/case.

⚠️ Non-negotiables carried from this session: any probe binary gets the two-arm
byte-identity gate (`l252_identity.py --probe X --flags Y`) before it is trusted —
for an *instrument* arm B must PASS, for a *mechanism* arm B must FAIL or it is a
silent no-op; any new mechanism ships behind a code default with a kill switch,
because the grader strips `ICCAD_*` (L158); and quality claims need OOS s1 **and**
s2.

### 4b. And why L256's failure points somewhere specific

L256 ruined ~12 % of the design and re-placed it **with the same greedy that had
just jammed**. That is why it only ever went ~2 % deep. L253 says the topology is
already right and L254 says the jam is fragmentation — so the missing piece is
**not a new topology search** (M27's sequence-pair / B\*-tree, judged not worth it
because the greedy is on the (area, HPWL) frontier) but a **recreate that can
place into fragmented space**. That is a small combinatorial problem — 10–15 items
into a known free region — and nothing in the ledger has attacked it.

The next probe therefore bounds the recreate before building one: **at the moment
the greedy jams, is there ANY legal placement of the remaining blocks in the free
space?** Bounded backtracking answers it.

* YES on most cases ⇒ a stronger recreate exists, and L255's curve prices the
  target (re-place ~10 % → s ≈ 1.06 → **+3.23 %**).
* NO ⇒ the jam is not repairable *given that prefix*, and the fix has to change
  the prefix — which is full re-placement, i.e. M27, where L129's from-scratch
  attempt reached 1.745 against the shipped 1.237.

⚠️ A NO does not close the axis (a different prefix might be placeable); a YES
opens a concrete mechanism immediately. Asymmetric, cheap, and the instrument
already exists (`constructive_l254.exe` reports the jam state).

### 4c. It was run — the answer is NO, and it is sharper than expected (L259)

`L259_REPORT.md`. Brute-forced on a 600×600 raster (which validates itself: its
occupancy matches exact area occupancy to 0.1 pp):

| case | placed | occupancy | biggest unplaced | free positions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| n=120 | 108 | 77.8 % | **23.0 × 21.0** | **0** |
| | | | 21.6 × 21.6 | 12 |
| | | | 15.1 × 15.1 | 1539 |
| n=120 | 119 | 83.2 % | **25.0 × 14.0** | **0** |
| n=119 | 117 | 80.2 % | **21.0 × 16.0** | **0** |
| | | | 8.5 × 8.5 | 6609 |

🔑 **The largest remaining block has nowhere at all to go; smaller ones have
thousands of spots.** This sharpens L254: `free/need ≥ 10×` is true *by area*, but
the largest contiguous free rectangle is smaller than the largest remaining
block. **Area was never the binding constraint — contiguity is.**

⇒ **"Bolt a stronger recreate onto the greedy" is closed.** Given the prefix the
greedy has committed, no placement algorithm finishes the layout — exact search,
MIP, skyline-with-backtracking all face zero legal positions for the block that
matters. That is why L256 stalled at ~2 % and why L258's better rule could not
rescue it.

**Still open**: a ruin set chosen to free a *contiguous* region big enough for the
big blocks, rather than L256's "overflow plus neighbours toward the far corner".
That is M27's multi-pair coordinated move, the thing M64 deferred and L129
attacked from scratch. The target is unchanged (**+3.23 %** at ~10 % re-placed)
and so is the shape of the answer: **a placer that decides where the big blocks go
before the small ones fill the gaps.**

🚨 Two wrong answers came first, both of which looked like findings — see
`L259_REPORT.md` §3. A sparse candidate set (per-rectangle corners only; the
complete bottom-left set is the **cross product** `{0}∪{right edges} × {0}∪{top
edges}`) returned NO after 1 node on every case. And a diagnostic that computed
max free *width* and max free *height* independently reported a 71.9×39.8 slot for
a 25.0×14.0 block, flatly contradicting the solver. Neither was trustworthy; the
raster settled it with no cleverness at all.

| **L266** | does `tuned` transfer? | **No.** sign flips in **both** split schemes; ≈0 on the heaviest 20 cases |

**The frame axis closes on a dilemma**: the only quality-robust ladder (`dense`,
positive on all four halves: +0.350 / +1.121 / +0.826 / +0.628) is unaffordable,
and every affordable ladder is sample-specific. L252's **+1.50 % upper bound
stands, and is now measured to be uncollectable** rather than merely bounded.

Findings worth carrying:

* 🚨 **`s_min` is a property of the greedy rule, not the instance.** Eviction
  moves it. It still does not become score — density was never the whole story:
  area improves and hpwl + vrel both pay it back (L251's coupling, measured).
* 🚨 **A weighted mean can improve while the count goes the wrong way.** L263's
  isolated eviction reads −0.52 % weighted but 75/91 on count: a few large wins
  against many small losses. That is a lottery, not a mechanism, and the
  portfolio does not collect it.
* 🚨 **A transfer headline can read 100 % while the candidate you care about is
  flipping sign.** L266's selection test picks `dense` (genuinely stable) and
  therefore reports perfect transfer — while `tuned`, the actual candidate,
  flips in both splits. **Read the per-arm per-half table, not the headline.**
* 🚨 **Failed frames are not cheap.** L254 measured a failing pack as having
  already placed ~91 % of blocks, and `max_trials` counts only *successes*, so
  every rung below `s_min` is paid in full. That is why 26 rungs costs 1.48× on
  the max-setter.
* **12 cases is not 40.** The dense ladder read −1.68 % on 12 and −0.74 % on 40.
  It was flagged unsafe when reported, and it was.
